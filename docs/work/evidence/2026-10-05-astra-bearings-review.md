Significant improvement in useful work at equal quality is **not established**. E6 supplies costly historical examples with incomplete measures, not a comparable before/after result. 2. **Have we gotten sidelined on a too-specific sub-project?** **Yes, with moderate confidence:** recurring internal census, publishing, guard and cleanup work occupies the evidence while the owner's underlying problem remains unsettled (E1, E5, E8). Some of that work was explicitly authorized and necessary. The inference is about the portfolio and its acceptance criteria, not misconduct by those lanes or proof that those capabilities should disappear.

3. **Have we spent time on a castle of patches instead of architecture and simplification?** **Yes in identifiable seams, not proven for the entire repository.** Owner-input and state-location failures recur after local repairs (E5, E8). This is sufficient to demand a class-level design explanation before another repair there. Continue retirement is direct contrary evidence to a universal accusation (E3). The raw audit's keyword lane counts are overlapping topic counts, so I do not treat them as numbers of architectural failures.

4. **Are we still building toward the simplest solution to the actual core problem?** **That has not yet been demonstrated.** The new no-code bet creates room to do it, but the old card and current owner intent differ, and no candidate has outcome evidence. A smaller file count or fewer hosts would not by itself answer this question. Simplicity must include the human handoffs, retained benefits, authority boundaries and failure recovery needed for real work, not just the code left in this repo.
